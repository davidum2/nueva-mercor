Extend CableSpan with Coulomb capstan friction over wrapped obstacle surfaces, distributing tension according to contact wrap and sliding direction. Changing friction parameters must not change the cable path, contact status, or curve arc lengths. The change belongs in the existing CableSpan sources, `Simbody/include/simbody/internal/CableSpan.h` and `Simbody/src/CableSpan.cpp`; do not add source files or touch the build configuration.

Each obstacle defaults to zero friction. Add `void setObstacleFrictionCoefficient(CableSpanObstacleIndex ix, Real value)` to configure an obstacle's friction, throwing an exception for a negative value and leaving the obstacle's previous friction value unchanged. Add `Real getObstacleFrictionCoefficient(CableSpanObstacleIndex ix) const` to query it.

For any obstacle curve segment, add `Real calcCurveSegmentWrapAngle(const State& state, CableSpanObstacleIndex ix) const`. It requires Stage::Position and returns the wrap angle of the geodesic curve segment, defined as the integral along the segment of the surface normal curvature in the path tangent direction. The curvature is signed: positive where the surface is convex along the path and negative where it is concave. A cable wrapped over a sphere or cylinder therefore has a positive wrap angle, while a stretch of contact along a concave part of a surface, such as the inner rim of a torus, counts negatively and can make the wrap angle negative. If the obstacle is not in contact with the path, it returns NaN.

Tension propagates from origin to termination following the Euler-Eytelwein capstan relation. Across each in-contact obstacle segment with wrap angle theta and Coulomb friction mu, tension multiplies from entry to exit by exp(mu * theta) when lengthening (cable length rate > 0), by exp(-mu * theta) when shortening (cable length rate < 0), and is unchanged when the length rate is zero. Only the sign of the length rate matters, however small its magnitude. The wrap angle enters with its sign: across a contact with a negative wrap angle the tension falls from entry to exit when lengthening and rises when shortening. Obstacles not in contact contribute no tension change, and via points are frictionless with continuous tension across them. When origin tension is non-positive, tension is zero everywhere along the path.

Add `Real calcTensionAtTermination(const State& state, Real originTension) const` to return the propagated tension at termination, and `Real calcFrictionPowerDissipation(const State& state, Real originTension) const` to return the mechanical power dissipated by friction. When no contact has a negative wrap angle, this is the cable length rate multiplied by the difference between termination tension and origin tension, and it is never negative. Both methods require Stage::Velocity and return 0 when originTension is non-positive.

Add `void applyBodyForcesWithFriction(const State& state, Real originTension, Vector_<SpatialVec>& bodyForcesInG) const`, which requires Stage::Velocity and accumulates spatial forces and moments into `bodyForcesInG` using the local tension profile. Tensile force on the origin body is scaled by originTension, tensile force on the termination body is scaled by termination tension, and each via point experiences force scaled by its local continuous tension. For each in-contact obstacle, the spatial wrench has Ground force `T_exit * t_exit - T_entry * t_entry` and moment about the obstacle body origin `r_exit % (T_exit * t_exit) - r_entry % (T_entry * t_entry)` (where `r` and `t` are contact position and unit tangent in Ground, and `T_entry` and `T_exit` are local entry and exit tensions). With zero friction, for each body the norm of the difference between the spatial wrench accumulated by this method and by `applyBodyForces` is less than `1e-12`. Non-positive originTension applies no forces.
